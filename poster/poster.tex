%% ── Problem ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Problem}

A \textbf{knowledge graph} (KG) is a network of facts stored as triples
\textbf{(subject, relation, object)}.
\textbf{Link prediction} asks: given $(h, r, ?)$, which entity $t$ is
most likely to complete the triple?

\vspace{0.25cm}

\vspace{0.2cm}
Models learn by comparing correct triples against \textit{negatives}
(fabricated wrong triples). The quality of negatives is critical.

%% ── Motivation ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Motivation}

\textbf{Random negatives} (gray box above) become trivial after a few
epochs: the model rejects them easily, so the \textbf{gradient signal
vanishes} and learning plateaus.

\vspace{0.18cm}
\textbf{Hard negatives} (amber box) keep the model challenged, but
training only on them can destabilise learning.

\vspace{0.18cm}
\textbf{Our research question.} Can a \textbf{mixture} of random and
hard negatives combine the accuracy of hard with the stability of
random --- and what is the best proportion?

%% ── Methodology ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Methodology}

{\small For each positive triple at training time:}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  >=Stealth, font=\small,
  box/.style={draw=secblue!70, fill=iceblue, rounded corners=5pt,
              inner xsep=8pt, inner ysep=6pt,
              minimum height=1.3cm, minimum width=4.0cm,
              text width=3.8cm, align=center},
  arr/.style={->, navy!80, line width=1.5pt},
  stg/.style={font=\scriptsize\bfseries, text=secblue}
]
  \node[box] (pool)  {Draw \textbf{64}\\candidates\\(random corruption)};
  \node[box, right=0.9cm of pool]  (score) {Score each\\with model\\$f_\theta$};
  \node[box, right=0.9cm of score] (sel)   {Select \textbf{8}\\by strategy};
  \node[box, right=0.9cm of sel]   (loss)  {Compute\\NSSA loss\\back-prop};
  \draw[arr] (pool.east) -- (score.west);
  \draw[arr] (score.east) -- (sel.west);
  \draw[arr] (sel.east) -- (loss.west);
  \node[stg, above=0.18cm of pool]  {Stage 1};
  \node[stg, above=0.18cm of score] {Stage 2};
  \node[stg, above=0.18cm of sel]   {Stage 2};
  \node[stg, above=0.18cm of loss]  {Stage 2};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.25cm}
{\small
\textbf{Selection strategies tested in Stage~2:}\\[5pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Random}: sample 8 uniformly (control)\\[3pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Hard}: take the 8 highest-scoring wrong candidates\\[3pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Mixed $p$}: take fraction $p$ hard + $(1-p)$ random,
  with $p\in\{30\%,\,50\%,\,70\%\}$\\[8pt]
\textbf{All strategies share the same Stage~1 pool: zero extra compute.}\\[5pt]
Common setup: RotatE, embedding dim~128, batch~1024, Adam lr$=10^{-3}$,
50~epochs (patience~10), seed~42.
}

%% ── Conclusions ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{7pt}
  \item \textbf{Hard negatives are a free upgrade:} same training pipeline,
    smarter selection step, +10.4\% MRR gain.
  \item \textbf{You don't need 100\% hard:} Mixed 30\% (2 hard examples
    out of 8) recovers 95\% of the gain with added stability.
  \item \textbf{Rare and ambiguous facts benefit most,} which is exactly
    where KG completion is hardest and most valuable in practice.
  \item \textbf{Future work:} dynamically adjust the hard fraction during
    training; scale to Wikidata-5M; test on RESCAL and TuckER.
\end{itemize}
