\secheader{Problem}

A \textbf{knowledge graph} (KG) is a network of facts stored as triples
\textbf{(subject, relation, object)}.
\textbf{Link prediction} asks: given $(h, r, ?)$, which entity $t$ is
most likely to complete the triple?

%% ── Motivation ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Motivation}

<<<<<<< HEAD
\textbf{Random negatives} are too easy: the model rejects them
quickly and stops improving.

\vspace{0.18cm}
\textbf{Hard negatives} carry a stronger signal, but always picking
the same plausible-but-wrong cases could over-focus training.

\vspace{0.18cm}
\textbf{Question:} would a \textbf{mix} of random and hard combine
both effects, and at what proportion?
=======
\textbf{Random negatives} become trivial after a few epochs: the model
rejects them easily, so the \textbf{gradient signal vanishes} and
learning plateaus.

\vspace{0.12cm}
\textbf{Hard negatives} keep the model challenged throughout training,
but using them exclusively can destabilise learning.
\textbf{A mixed strategy} combines both.

\vspace{0.12cm}
\textbf{Question:} What mixture gives the best accuracy--stability
trade-off on FB15k-237?

  \node[stg, above=0.18cm of loss]  {Stage 2};
\end{tikzpicture}

\vspace{0.25cm}
{\small
\textbf{Selection strategies tested in Stage~2:}\\[5pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Random}: sample 8 uniformly (control)\\[3pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Hard}: take the 8 highest-scoring wrong candidates\\[3pt]
\hspace*{6pt}$\bullet$~\textbf{Mixed $p$}: take fraction $p$ hard + $(1-p)$ random,
  with $p\in\{30\%,\,50\%,\,70\%\}$\\[8pt]
\textbf{All strategies share the same Stage~1 pool: zero extra compute.}\\[5pt]
Common setup: RotatE, embedding dim~128, batch~1024, Adam lr$=10^{-3}$,
50~epochs (patience~10), seed~42.
}

%% ── Conclusions ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\secheader{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
  \item \textbf{Hard negatives are a free upgrade:} same pipeline,
    smarter selection step, +10.4\% MRR.
  \item \textbf{You don't need 100\% hard:} Mixed 30\% (2 of 8 hard)
    recovers 95\% of the gain with added stability.
  \item \textbf{Gains are largest on data-sparse slices (rare
    relations +3.6\,pp, low tail in-degree +3.0\,pp):} sparse training
    data leaves embeddings under-trained, so each hard-negative step
    is more informative.
  \item \textbf{Head prediction stays $\sim$2$\times$ harder
    ($\sim$0.18 vs.\ $\sim$0.39 MRR):} FB15k-237 is dominated by
    \textbf{N-to-1} relations (e.g.\ \texttt{/film/film/genre}), so
    many heads compete for the same tail. This is a dataset bias no
    sampling rule fixes.
\end{itemize}
